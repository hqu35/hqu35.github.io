Course Experiences

MATH:234

The course is the epilogue in the standard calculus sequence, which focuses on multivariable calculus. We learned some basic differential geometry, partial derivatives, and finally double and triple integrals. Overall speaking, this course inherits the style in the calculus sequenece that only requires students to do basic calculation.

MATH:340

This course focuses on basic matrix algebra, with most of the proofs omitted. Just like how math 234 works, the course only expects students to do calculation instead of diving deep into the proofs of theorems. Though the disadvantage is pretty obvious: one might forget the knowledge pretty soon after the final since no proofs are provided to really get you understand what's really under the hood.

Math 521

It was such a horrible experience when I took this course, the lecturer just copeid and pasted whatever's in her course notes, without providing any motivation nor explanation. But luckily, the exams were just asking us to reproduce the proofs of the main theorems in the textbook. The course mostly covered chapter 1 to chapter 8 in Rudin's Principle of Mathemtical Analysis. We learned basic point set topology(which is not beginner friendly), continuous functions, differentiation & Integration, Sequence and Series, Uniform Convergence, and some applications of these knowledge. I strongly recommend you preview chapter 2 in Rudin's book if you plan to take this course, as it'll become a pain in the ass......

Math 541

I took the course with professor Milkahil Ivanov, who's strict and rigorous. We covered primarily group theory, and a bit of Ring theory. The first half of the course was relatively manageable. Everything became harsh and bad after the second midterm(Roughly speaking, quotient groups, orbits, direct products, etc). Overall speaking, the exams except the first midterm were hard, I did bad on the second midterm, but managed to get the second highest score on the final and the first midterm.

Math 561

I took the course with professor Tullia Dymarz, who's passionate all the time giving lectures. We followed closely the book Elementary Differential Geoemetry by Pressley. At the end of the course, we had a bit taste of hyperbolic geometry, which is pretty fun. Overall a pretty chilly class.
